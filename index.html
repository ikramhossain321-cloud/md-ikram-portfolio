About Me

I am a dedicated public policy researcher with over eight years of experience in policy formulation and implementation, now focusing on the intersection of AI Governance, Ethics, and Sociotechnical Systems. As an incoming Ph.D. student at the University of Houston, my research centers on the "Safe Usage of AI" and the development of trustworthy frameworks that enhance institutional accountability. My work specializes in identifying systemic vulnerabilities and mitigating risks through advanced early warning systems (EWS) and human-in-the-loop oversight. By bridging the gap between technical innovation and regulatory requirements, particularly in high-stakes areas like healthcare fraud detection, I aim to develop scalable, governance-ready architectures that ensure AI remains transparent, secure, and aligned with public interest.

Prior to advancing my academic career into AI governance, I served in high-impact leadership and policy analytical roles with UNICEF and BRAC, Bangaldesh. By spearheading community engagement strategies and directing complex, data-driven programmatic evaluations, often involving thousands of vulnerable demographics in real-time crisis scenarios, I developed a profound understanding of how policies actually land on the ground. Today, I utilize advanced quantitative/qualitative analytics (Python, R, Stata) to investigate and design adaptable AI frameworks. Whether tackling generative AI applications in healthcare fraud detection, assessing education analytics paradigms, or evaluating socio-technological impacts on public trust, my goal remains constant: bridging the gap between rigorous empirical data and equitable, human-centric policy reform.

2026 Health Information Science and Systems

A Generative AI-Enhanced Sociotechnical Framework (FRAIG) for Healthcare Fraud Detection: A Systematic Review and Evidence-Based Design (Under Review)

Afsana Munni, M. I. H. Bhuiyan, et al.

Healthcare fraud detection presents significant challenges due to highly imbalanced datasets, evolving fraud patterns, and increasing regulatory requirements. While artificial intelligence (AI) approaches have demonstrated strong predictive performance, existing studies often lack methodological standardization, interpretability, and real-world applicability. This study presents a systematic review of 59 studies on AI-based healthcare fraud detection, identifying key methodological trends, performance characteristics, and critical limitations. The findings reveal a shift from traditional supervised learning approaches toward hybrid, deep learning, and graph-based models, alongside persistent challenges related to class imbalance, explainability, temporal validation, and deployment readiness. Based on these insights, we propose the FRAIG (Fraud Detection with Responsible AI and Generative Intelligence) framework, a novel sociotechnical architecture that integrates generative AI capabilities into healthcare fraud detection systems. FRAIG incorporates a generative augmentation layer for synthetic data generation and automated explanation, hybrid human-AI validation, interpretability-first model design, and regulatory compliance mechanisms aligned with healthcare standards. The framework is further supported by a practical use case and an evidence-based mapping of framework components to the literature, highlighting both well-established areas and critical research gaps. By bridging technical innovation with institutional and ethical considerations, FRAIG provides a comprehensive and adaptive approach to healthcare fraud detection. The study contributes to the literature by advancing sociotechnical systems theory through the integration of generative AI and by offering a scalable, regulation-aware framework for real-world deployment. Future research should focus on standardized evaluation protocols, empirical validation in operational settings, and the integration of privacy-preserving and adaptive learning techniques.
